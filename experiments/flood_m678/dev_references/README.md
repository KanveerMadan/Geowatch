# Midland development reference

The Tittabawassee River peaked at the Midland gauge between 16:15 and 16:45 UTC on May 20, 2020, based on the observations bracketing its maximum. Landsat 8 observed the reach at 16:21:46 UTC. The before image was acquired May 4 on the same path and row. The U.S. Geological Survey independently identified these dates for its comparison of Midland before and after the Edenville and Sanford failures. Heavy rain contributed to this flood. [USGS before and after](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/eros/dam-breaks-michigan) · [NWS incident account](https://www.weather.gov/dtx/HistoricFlooding-May-17-20-2020)

The 30 m NPZ uses the flood-speed four-state encoding. The two binary masks can be reconstructed exactly from state. In this development tile 133 cells show newly visible open water, 2,874 show visible dry land on both dates, 6,967 are ambiguous and 60,782 are unobserved or excluded. Only 3,007 of 70,756 cells support a wet/dry comparison. Keep the denominator when reporting coverage. Many areas near structures, beneath trees or among green crops cannot be judged.

Water needs agreement between the USGS pixel QA water flag and the sign of the modified normalized difference water index. A clear beforehand land observation is required. Open-ground classes come from the pre-event 2019 NLCD cover. Snow, cloud, shadow, saturation, missing water-history coverage, permanent water and obstructing land-cover classes cannot supply dry evidence. Greenness above GeoWatch's existing NDVI 0.3 cutoff at either observation makes the result ambiguous. The permanent-water mask uses the round-3 JRC occurrence rule, which includes post-2020 observations. The two optical signals share the same instrument and the gauge validates timing only. Independent cell-level accuracy and attribution to breach versus rainfall are undetermined.

Regenerate from the repository Python environment with `python midland_reference.py CACHE` in the parent directory. The reader only accepts the fixed May 2020 DEV pair. It makes a single bounded Earth Engine request under the existing shared lock when the source cache is empty. The JSON receipt records the pixel grid, acquisition, algorithm fingerprint, state counts and payload checksum.
