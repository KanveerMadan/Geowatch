# M6–M8 v2 | Conditional flood extent

**Build a reach-based water-spreading model for M7 and M8. Give each mechanism its own way to estimate water level and where water enters or backs up. Keep M6 as an experimental scenario with separate evidence for water and debris.** Share training only where the physical process and the observations agree. Each type must earn its own validation.

## What the map promises

Given an identified incident, source location, time window and forcing available at issuance, rank observable land cells by potential inundation near the local crest. Return the forcing source, its timestamp, reach, terrain resolution, coverage, limitations and model version. A score is an ordering. The study does not estimate the probability a structure will fail or a jam will form. A user-entered release produces a scenario. A gauge crest supplied after the fact produces a hindcast. Keep these distinct from an operational prediction. Record observation time and publication time for every input, including terrain, structures and retrospective discharge. Evaluate the intended product with the same input availability. If a hindcast wins, only the hindcast earns that scope.

## How a change in forcing changes the map

1. Build a directed reach graph upstream and downstream of the source. Split it at confluences, dams, breaches, constrictions and major slope changes. Use the best legally usable terrain and surveyed structures available at issue time. Align horizontal and vertical datums, represent height error and keep the native support of coarse forcing. Missing terrain corrections, bathymetry or barrier heights widen uncertainty. Missing barrier inventory cannot establish an open path.
2. For each graph segment estimate an event water surface W(r,t) in the terrain datum. Use an applicable local stage record or rating curve first, then a regional curve with uncertainty. A HAND cross-section approximation can tabulate wetted area A and hydraulic radius R versus stage and invert Manning's Q = A R^(2/3) sqrt(S)/n for ordinary open-channel flow. Check against an independent gauge or high-water profile. A stage estimate from the gauge used to timestamp labels is not independent stage validation.
3. Seed only the hydraulically appropriate source cells. Let B(i,r) be the lowest spill elevation along an admissible route to cell i (the minimum over paths of their maximum terrain or barrier elevation). Open a mapped breach only in the failed scenario. For locally level floodplain compartments, potential depth is max(0, W − z_i) where W reaches B. Propagate arrivals along the graph, cap storage and outflow by the available hydrograph volume, and use time-varying stages across reaches. Never fill every connected hollow instantaneously. Route backwater upstream separately. Test tributaries, disconnected basins, opposite banks, finite-volume pulses and datum shifts on synthetic terrain before fitting. A constant stage minus HAND within a single reach reproduces the HAND ranking. Connected paths, changing stage profiles and finite storage are the proposed sources of spatial skill.
4. The first candidate is this physics score alone. A second learns a bounded correction from shared DEV events using the existing anchored learner and nested regional folds. Inputs include connected depth, overtopping margin, arrival relative to crest, volume-limited storage, channel distance and uncertainty. Keep a physics-only fallback. Train with balanced incident/cluster influence; learn region effects from measured characteristics (slope, confinement, floodplain storage, snow regime), never place names. Select correction strength in inner folds. Compare target-only, river transfer, and river plus eligible flash transfer. Remove flash if transfer degrades target DEV performance. Report held-out basins, poor-source settings and the fraction abstaining alongside skill. A strong model requiring a gauge has a gauged-reach scope.
5. On the same DEV boxes, compare actual forcing with seasonal/default forcing and forcing exchanged between matched incidents. Also remove barriers and travel-time restrictions one at a time. Assess within-event rank changes and whether stage/extent responds in the correct direction. If real forcing improves stage but leaves ranks unchanged, document that physical improvement and stop the validation bid for spatial skill.

## Mechanism adapters

| Mode | Input and physical treatment | Principal failure and decisive DEV check |
|---|---|---|
| M7 reservoir breach | Locate failure, failure time and downstream path. Use measured release if available at the declared issue time. Otherwise estimate a family of breach hydrographs using head above the breach invert and **releasable** volume. Use a documented empirical peak-flow relation in its material and unit domain (e.g. Froehlich 1995b); 2008 breach geometry/time regressions require a separate outflow calculation. Route inflow, attenuation and travel time, conserving released volume. | Inventory capacity may greatly exceed the volume released. Reject impossible volume integrals, compare arrival and peak stage with downstream gauges withheld from forcing, and check connected extent versus terrain. Unknown failure size gives a scenario range. [USACE breach equations](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/rasdocs/ras1dtechref/6.5/performing-a-dam-break-study-with-hec-ras/estimating-dam-breach-parameters/estimating-breach-parameters) |
| M7 levee breach | Use river stage at the breach, failure side and opening time. Discharge through the opening depends on effective width and positive head to the receiving compartment; reduce exchange as heads equalize. Route into the protected side. Use US National Levee Database, national inventories, surveyed crest lines and OSM where verified. | OSM lines may lack elevation or have false gaps. Independently check bank side, intact control segments and breach-open versus intact extents. Never use an event footprint to infer where to open the levee for its own test. [National Levee Database](https://levees.sec.usace.army.mil/) |
| M8 sustained melt | Aggregate antecedent ERA5-Land SWE, melt and rain over contributing areas with travel-time lags. MODIS snow-cover retreat checks timing; snow cover alone cannot supply SWE. Use gauged flow or GEOGloWS/GloFAS routed discharge with a return period based on earlier years of that same series. Convert to reach-specific stage. Separate rain-on-snow as a mixed driver. | Coarse products miss local melt and regulation. Compare observed versus modelled discharge, arrival and spatial skill; hold out whole basins, and compare against seasonal discharge. [ERA5-Land](https://cds.climate.copernicus.eu/datasets/reanalysis-era5-land) · [GEOGloWS](https://www.geoglows.org/) |
| M8 ice jam | Require independently located obstruction and breakup interval. With gauge pairs or ice observations, estimate upstream stage surcharge and distance of backwater; after release route a downstream pulse. With only channel geometry, supply alternative jam-location scenarios. | Open-water rating curves can miss a large stage increase at ordinary flow. Fit surcharge on DEV gauge profiles, check upstream/downstream asymmetry and timing on different rivers. Do not infer blockage from test water pixels. [Canadian River Ice Database](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/12/1835/2020/) |
| M6 GLOF and debris | Start with independently sourced release volume and outlet from GLOF v4.2 or event reports. Track measured released volume, inferred release fraction and area-derived estimate separately. Route water-dominated outbursts as finite pulses. For documented debris-rich cases use a LAHARZ-style cross-section A = c V^(2/3), constrained to a routed corridor and runout budget. Use published coefficients as priors; fit only on independent DEV valleys with the matching material. | Sediment deposits and peak water are different targets. Separate field/aerial deposit boundaries from wet/dry imagery; snow, shadow and radar layover abstain. Test footprint and runout by held-out valley, compare diminishing volume and branching paths. One Iceland winter case cannot calibrate global coefficients. Ship each mode as a scenario until its own evidence qualifies. [Pinned GLOF catalogue](https://zenodo.org/records/23370149) |

Where causes coexist, run the supported adapters with a shared time axis and combine inflows and boundary conditions before routing. Preserve uncertainty about which driver contributed. Do not add probabilities or double-count the same water from melt and modelled discharge. Generic water labels test extent, never causal attribution.

## Event supply and observations

The following are **recruitment budgets**, not verified counts. In a first metadata sweep, screen roughly 100 M7 leads (about half levees), 120 M8 leads (split between sustained melt and jams), and 60 distinct M6 lake/valley leads. Start with documented breaches from national incident investigations and DFO; intersect with EMS, UNOSAT and Copernicus GFM acquisition metadata. Start ice searches with USACE CRREL and Canadian hydrometric ice flags, adding Nordic national gauge services. Use USGS and national water agencies for local crest brackets. Search GLOF v4.2 across mountain regions and include Norwegian NVE reports. EMS/GFM incidents, agency reports and gauges frequently refer to the same storm. Join before counting. No archive is presumed globally complete. [CRREL inventory](https://icejam.sec.usace.army.mil/ords/icejam/r/icejam/home) · [NVE](https://glacier.nve.no/Glacier/viewer/GLOF/enno/) · [GFM observation masks](https://extwiki.eodc.eu/gfm_assets/gfm4.0_pum_2025.pdf)

For scale, a hypothetical 25% yield after independence, timing and labels would give 25 M7, 30 M8 and 15 M6 usable clusters from those budgets, across DEV and reserve combined. Replace that assumption with a stratified DEV screening yield and uncertainty. At that yield, even 40 usable test clusters alone require about 160 independent leads earmarked for testing, plus separate DEV supply. Record all losses by region and mechanism. Historic reports predating usable imagery help engineering case studies but cannot automatically enter pixel evaluation.

Generate references by the frozen five-state scheme. Prefer clear optical with independently observed pre-event land; use same-orbit S1 fill only under valid observation geometry. Preserve seasonal water, snow, built-up, ambiguous and blind masks and the existing eligibility rules. Record each cell's source and offset from the **local** crest; do not silently replace the target with a union of observations from different flood phases. Lock the primary temporal merge on DEV; retain v1's timing limits as outer bounds. Use independent high-water marks for point checks in a common datum; absence of marks supplies no dry labels. Blind-review wet and dry examples, including paddy and shadow. GFM and S1 derived from the same acquisition supply one observation family. For release forecasts prohibit future imagery, realized peaks and as-built post-failure surveys from predictor inputs.

## What can earn a pass

Use a reach-event as the **routing** unit. Keep the primary round-3-style within-event pixel AUC, 8 km scored footprint, 30 m grid, 50 wet and 50 dry minimum, paired common support, unit averaging convention, 990 m paired block and cluster bootstrap. Route beyond the footprint to avoid boundary effects. Treat source-located corridors as a predeclared secondary diagnosis; do not substitute whichever footprint scores better. Group the transitive closure of common storm, reach, lake, cascade and repeated gauge across all study branches. Block whole related regions in transfer CV. No convenient reach subdivisions increase sample size.

For each type require mean AUC ≥0.70, ≥5 usable independent clusters and a 95% lower bound on paired gain strictly above zero against the stronger of elevation and HAND, selected by the higher mean on identical eligible units as in the pinned scorer. Remove its baseline-only exception. Equality fails. Do not select the stronger baseline separately per pixel or event. Freeze the exact bootstrap, seeds, weighting and tie handling. Publish both contrasts. Candidate missing values cannot quietly select an easier population; predefine mandatory coverage, common support and dropout rules, and report comparison with full baseline coverage. Never use reserved labels to decide power or eligibility policy.

Plan separate M7 and M8 tests, with submode results and coverage always shown. Training together offers transfer without changing the claim. A pooled test could only validate a separately defined conditional spreading product with a fixed deployment mixture; it cannot confer M7 or M8 validation. Defer such a test unless registered before a different fresh reserve. Keep both v1 tags and their reserved clusters quarantined indefinitely. No v1 one-shot.

Use 34–42 usable independent clusters **per tested type** as an initial recruitment target. The v1 power figures measure another contrast. Re-estimate power on out-of-fold DEV predictions for the actual model vs stronger terrain baseline; simulate the entire joint pass rule, dropout and cluster structure. Require approximately 0.80 or better under a conservative plausible DEV scenario before opening the fresh test. If plausible gain is zero or mean AUC falls below the floor, adding events alone cannot rescue the model. There is no defensible empirical percentage chance of passing today. M7 has the best engineering prospect on documented breaches; M8 melt is plausible on gauged rivers, ice jam weaker without local stage; M6 global validation has the weakest evidence.
