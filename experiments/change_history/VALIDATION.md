# GeoWatch change detection validation

**Public alerts should stay off.** The locked evaluations found useful detections of vegetation loss and water loss, substantial misses in new areas, and poor support for building-area estimates. No single accuracy percentage describes these tests. A cell, a tracked building, a field inspection and a distinct incident have different denominators.

| Test | What GeoWatch detected | What limits the result |
|---|---|---|
| Forest and water, Sentinel-2 against independent Landsat products | 43/99 forest-loss, 22/33 water-loss and 0/8 water-gain cells | Only one reservoir supplied gain cases. Forest results ranged from 20/24 in Pará to 0/24 in Sumatra. In Chaco, 13/24 losses went unconfirmed despite continuous coverage. |
| Wildfires, independent Landsat burn-severity maps | 8/10 verified high-severity cells across ten US wildfires, median confirmation 18 days after incident ignition among the eight detected | Seven original reference cells required a 100 m correction, documented before interpreting scores. Each fire supplied one cell. [Verification and outcomes](FIRE_VALIDATION.md) |
| Brazilian clearing notices | 14/20 selected cells with the default sensors, 12/20 with Sentinel-2 alone | Three clusters, all large clear-cuts. Of 1,598 eligible reference locations in the registry, 1,578 were outside the locked test. Notice dates establish when clearing was observed. Exact local onset is unknown. |
| Bangladesh monsoon flood | 0/120 registered water-positive cells by the June 18, 2022 survey and 0/120 by June 30 | Across all 120 there was no usable optical water-index observation in June on the required support. The default configuration includes radar, but its backscatter changes alone cannot confirm a water gain. One incident accounts for all cells. |
| Ordinary monitoring, Sentinel-2 | 26 confirmed cell-months among 2,592 in six plots. Full observation coverage in 662. No matching directional confirmation among 83 changes in monthly water occupancy on independent maps. | Endpoint water occupancy can change seasonally. Blinded review found evidence of stable visible water occupancy for two of nine water confirmations. The other seven were unresolved. |
| Ordinary monitoring, default sources | 1 radar decrease in 576 cell-months across four other plots. 540 had full coverage in some signal, 359 had full water-index coverage. | The two ordinary samples used different locations and years. The physical cause of the radar decrease is unknown. The samples cannot establish false incidents per whole city-month. |
| Building additions and removals | All 334 surveyed blocks had radar. On the 181 blocks shared by all methods, combined mean absolute error was 152.63 m² for additions and 9.12 m² for removals. Predicting zero gave 91.69 m² and 8.61 m². | Site-weighted errors, four held-out regions, only six blocks with mapped removals. All 22 gain-positive blocks in Lusaka lacked common optical coverage. |
| Recent routine building gains and fire damage | On the Dutch common sample, combined gain ranking AUC was 0.52 with two positive and 25 negative blocks. In Pacific Palisades, combined loss ranking AUC was 0.65 with 90 destruction-positive and three inspected-undamaged blocks. | Few counterexamples in either check. Ranking cannot establish physical area accuracy. |

The monsoon reference was UNOSAT's June 18 RCM-1 radar delineation around Sylhet. All 25 sample points per selected 100 m cell had to fall inside mapped flood water. The test drew 120 of 294 eligible cells in the registered square. The reference is a preliminary satellite interpretation. It supplies an observation of inundation on one day. It does not date the onset of water in each cell. The June 18 replay was cut off mid-month, so its month-completeness flag cannot establish whether usable images existed by that day. An offline read of the archived June observations found six Sentinel-2 acquisition groups and one Landsat 9 group, with zero usable water-index measurements on those cells. Six Sentinel-1 groups supplied radar measurements for all 120 cells. This identifies a specific gap in converting observable radar changes into a dependable water-gain decision. [Reference and locked sample](monsoon_water_manifest.json) · [Per-cell observations and decisions](monsoon_water_result.json)

The first live silent run in Bandung recorded what was actually available through November 20, 2026. It produced zero candidate or confirmed changes in 36 cells. One collection verifies the live recording path. A prospective false-alert rate requires repeated collections and independent reviews. The historic experiments selected locations for particular settings. Counts from adjacent cells can reflect one disturbance. None of the experiments estimates global precision or a universal minimum detectable change size.

For the next development round, Chaco supplies examples where the optical measurements were available and the detector still missed a change. The Sylhet case supplies a different test for radar-based water interpretation when optical measurements are missing. For buildings, define additions and removals separately and train with more independently dated positive examples, particularly removals. Keep a simple zero-change predictor in every comparison. The published test cases have now been seen. Any claim that a revised method improves generalization needs newly reserved locations or incidents. Public alerts also need repeated prospective collections with independent review of both flagged and quiet locations.
