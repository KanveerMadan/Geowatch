# Round 2 prospective holdout

This design reserves the six named 24 km windows in fresh_plan.json and three deterministic SpaceNet sites. Only existing round-1 and development records informed geographic exclusions. Their rounded locations, including reference-only Amazon notices, MTBS candidates, ordinary monitoring neighborhoods, forest and water boxes, building sites and historic flood-study bounding boxes, are frozen in fresh_exclusions.json. Each named water or forest window centre is more than 100 km from the nearest point on this list. The source imagery, labels, event polygons, catalog counts and outcomes for the new cases remain unopened until the locked contents are published. Fresh here refers to project-level case history.

The runner seals code, data-selection rules and trained model with SHA256, verifies exact bytes against the registered Git commit, and verifies that commit against live origin/codex/change-history before opening any reference or predictor. A receipt records that successful remote verification time in UTC. Source failures can resume using the identical commit, source batches and completed site receipts. The final result can be produced once. The six nominal site frames and eligible counts stay in the result, including empty strata. No substitutions, test-driven method adjustments, or repeated test interpretations of a new model.

## Flood gain, new water, and water loss

The three frames span the 2020 Michigan dam failures (including reservoir drawdown), the 2022 Lismore flood and the 2024 Rio Grande do Sul flood. Dates in the JSON are monitoring boundaries. May 19 is the Michigan dam-failure day; the remaining dates anchor regional event windows and do not establish local cell onset. For each window compare 90 pre-window days with the monitoring window using USGS Landsat 8/9 Collection 2 QA_PIXEL water, excluding fill, cloud, cirrus, shadow, snow and radiometric saturation. A distinct day contributes wet or dry evidence only where an individual scene agrees across all 25 fixed reporting points in the 100 m square; overlapping same-day scenes vote once and conflicting wet/dry votes cancel. Gains require at least two dry pre-days and no wet pre-day, plus one or more later wet days. Loss reverses this definition. Temporary changes may qualify. Stable dry or wet controls require at least two agreeing days in each period with no opposite observed day. Missing dates and mixed cells never supply evidence for a dry classification. USGS CFMask provides the 30 m water bit. It is a satellite classification of visible open water. It cannot establish whether a flood caused the transition, or what happened between clear acquisitions. Repeated sampling at 20 m checks consistent support in the reporting cell. The 25 samples come from correlated native pixels. Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 supply the candidate measurements. Historical pre-event JRC information can share Landsat lineage with pre-state evidence. [USGS Landsat Collection 2 QA bands](https://www.usgs.gov/landsat-missions/landsat-collection-2-quality-assessment-bands)

Use up to 12 cells per stratum per site, chosen by reproducible SHA256 rank with at least 500 m separation within each stratum. This is purposive, class-balanced sampling. Evaluate gain against gains and stable dry controls, and loss against losses and stable wet controls. Do not infer an operational positive predictive value from the deliberately sampled proportions. Use all available 120 prior days of same-track history with the completed frozen water method. Count at most one matching confirmation per cell and target. Save the first confirmation, its lag since the monitoring window opened, and its signed difference in days from the first qualifying Landsat observation of the changed state. A negative difference means Sentinel confirmed the change before Landsat observed it. Neither interval measures delay from the actual local onset.

## Forest clearing and controls

Use 2023 stand-replacement loss at every one of 25 support points in three new regions of Indonesia, Cambodia and Peru. Use Hansen v1.12 land with initial canopy cover of at least 30%, a forest-domain choice. Stable controls require no mapped loss by end-2023 and Landsat median NDVI at least 0.5 in July–October of both 2022 and 2023 with an absolute difference at most 0.1. Each median needs at least two clear looks per sample point. These guards select visibly persistent green canopy and do not prove the absence of brief disturbance. Full support is required. The same label-blind hash and spacing rule selects up to 12 per class and region. Use Sentinel histories for 2021–2023. Count first confirmed loss inside 2023, without assigning day-scale delay to an annually dated reference. Hansen annual tree-cover loss can include fire, harvest and clearing. Its 30 m Landsat interpretation identifies substantial canopy loss but cannot determine the cause or reliably date a partial loss. The candidate uses Sentinel-1/2 change and an independent pre-monitoring 25 m JAXA PALSAR forest mask for 2020. Its forest requirement can omit plantation loss or recent forest growth. Such reference positives still enter all-positive recall. [Hansen v1.12](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/UMD_hansen_global_forest_change_2024_v1_12) [JAXA PALSAR forest map](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/JAXA_ALOS_PALSAR_YEARLY_FNF4)

## Independent building endpoints

After remote verification, order SpaceNet 7 train catalogue identifiers by SHA256 of "round2/" plus the identifier. Take the first three at least 200 km from every excluded point, the registered natural-area frames and previous selections, with a same-month one-year pair containing tracked footprints, unusable areas and georeferenced reference image. Use the latest eligible pair. Cap combined raw downloads at 32 MiB per candidate to bound resources. Only catalogue eligibility can skip a site. No selections based on content, model support or whether a city contains expansion. Save exact public object keys, ETags and byte sizes. The protocol locks the selection algorithm before opening the catalogue; site identities are defined by that algorithm.

Compare the trained building candidate on complete 200 m blocks within the 2 km centre frame. SpaceNet's independently mapped, tracked Planet footprints establish areas gained or lost by ID appearance/disappearance. Joint valid coverage and ambiguous object masks follow the sealed historical survey implementation. A direction is positive at 100 m² gross change or more and negative at exactly zero. Smaller positive areas remain ambiguous and unscored for that direction. Use the 15th of each survey month as satellite input anchor for the frozen building model; survey mosaic acquisition is not an exact single day. Evaluate additions and removals separately using the candidate's frozen operating point and ranking. Persistent-ID shape edits are outside this reference. Tracking errors can mimic removal. These historical labels test geographical transfer to the development settings represented in SpaceNet. A current-year construction or demolition rate requires a separate recent survey. ## Decision and limitations

For every target retain true/false confirmations, negatives, unobserved positives and negatives, unknown references, recall over all independent positives, recall on observable positives, false confirmation fraction on observable controls, and sample precision. Compare zero-change on identical support. Where both observed classes exist, report observed sensitivity minus the fraction of observed controls falsely flagged, by site and overall. Zero-change scores zero on this equal-class summary. Report the components alongside it because the operational costs can differ. Report building constant-score ranking alongside candidate ranking when both classes exist. All misses remain in overall recall. Counts refer to 100 or 200 m cells clustered by place. Equal class sampling and correlations between neighboring cells prevent city-wide or worldwide accuracy claims. Never authorize public alerts solely from this small holdout. Alert approval also requires independent review of actual repeated forward notifications at the promised geographic scale and an agreed operational false-alert budget.

Report failed and inconclusive outcomes for every predeclared direction. Promotion also requires reproducible improvement across separate incidents. One silent collection cannot establish sustained prospective burden.
